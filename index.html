The Chief Guard's briefing

Before the doors open

He has always loved them, and he has yearned for this day. Though they have never met him, he has seen them. He has beheld their hearts and discerned their fruit. Nothing has been hidden. Know that his judgment is just. None will be slighted or misrepresented.

The question put to every citizen

Prepare a full account of your stewardship, because you can no longer be my steward. Luke 16:2  ·  NABRE

A screen above the throne replays the citizen's entire life from the first day of school until the day before. Every deed. Every word. Every motive behind both. The whole assembly watches it with you.

No creature is concealed from him, but everything is naked and exposed to the eyes of him to whom we must render an account. Hebrews 4:13  ·  NABRE

The two destinations

After the review, the Chief Scribe is asked one question: is this name found in the Book of Life? Everything else follows from the answer.

Destination one

Affabel

The city itself. Citizenship for the rest of their life. But the parable is blunt that not all citizens are rewarded equally, and the difference is enormous.

  • A throne beside mine Underruler in the Regal Center, governing the city and the outer realms.
  • The mountains The midsection. Creative work, a good home, a real place.
  • The flatlands The least in the kingdom. Labor-intensive work, the back rows of the hall.
Destination two

The forsaken land of Lone

A windowless metal structure in the Great Desert of Fire, and one place beneath it. Utter darkness, hopelessness, and loneliness for the remainder of their life.

  • The lower level For those who never truly knew, yet still did what deserves exile.
  • The upper level Where the heat is greatest. For those who knew and refused.
  • The dungeon beneath Alone, near the sulfur rocks. The blackness of darkness, reserved forever.

How you will rule

Three decisions on every case. First the destination. Then the station or the measure. Then the hardest one: name the Scripture your judgment rests on. A verdict you cannot ground in the writings is not a judgment, it is an opinion.

One warning. The docket is deliberately shuffled. Do not assume the person in front of you came from the same hall as the last one, and do not assume the pattern you just spotted will hold.

    You have judged eleven people today

    “I am the searcher of hearts and minds and I will give each of you what your works deserve.” Revelation 2:23  ·  NABRE

    Every one of them was certain they knew how this would go. Two of them were right. The parable ends by handing the question back: if your own life were on that screen this afternoon, every deed and every word and the motive under each one, what would the assembly see?

    Beauty had been theirs for the choosing.
